Research

This page links my research interests in areas such as graph theory, statistics, econometrics, and cryptography. I have relied on AI and agentic tools, mostly Codex (from OpenAI) and OpenCode, a coding harness from Anomaly, in these works. Each page is usually multiple sessions to correct arrangement of topics, formatting, and visual aesthetics, often lasting eight hours, including reprompts. I manually check the results for accuracy and have spotted issues (not necessarily errors) in many sessions.

  • Budget Impact Analysis: An Application in Health Economics
    Budget impact analysis is used across diverse policy settings to evaluate the financial consequences of new programs, technologies, and interventions. This interactive study applies the framework to prescription drug reimbursement in British Columbia using a study from Canada's Drug Agency (CDA-AMC) as its main reference.
  • Measuring Uncertainty in Statistics Canada's Monthly Labour Force Survey (LFS)
    Explore LFS's sampling uncertainty, bootstrap variance estimation, confidence intervals, and the reliability of national and provincial employment and unemployment estimates, with an empirical analysis of September 2026.
  • Algorithms Behind City Traffic Lights
    A visual and mathematical journey from one four-way intersection to coordinated urban signal networks, covering phases, queues, detectors, timing, offsets, adaptive control, and network-level optimization.
  • The Enigma of Git Commands
    A visual and technical exploration of Git, from the working tree, staging area, commits, branches and remotes to Git's underlying object database, directed acyclic graph, hashes, references, merging, rebasing and recovery.
  • Mathematics of Poker (Texas Hold’em)
    A visual explanation of poker through combinatorics, probability, expected value, and reproducible Monte Carlo simulation.
  • Inside Matrix Multiplication — A Visual Investigation with Java
    An interactive investigation from visible row-column arithmetic and Java execution to geometry, memory tiles, sparse and parallel computation, Strassen recursion, floating-point reality, and the matrix multiplication exponent.
  • Introduction to Lean Programming Language
    A mathematical introduction to Lean 4, from algebra and induction to linear maps, calculus, differential equations, and the Fréchet chain rule, with proof-state walkthroughs and downloadable Lean examples.
  • Using GPT-6 Astra with Blender: A 3D Laboratory for Graph Theory, GIS, and Spatial Economics
    An editable procedural city becomes a visual laboratory for shortest paths, network resilience, Monte Carlo simulation, and transit accessibility, with Blender scenes, animations, interactive 3D models, and reproducible Python source.
  • The Riemann Zeta Function
    An interactive visual guide to the Riemann zeta function, from infinite series and Euler's prime product to complex zeros, the Riemann Hypothesis, zero statistics, and recent research.
  • From fMRI to Machine Learning: A Visual Guide to Brain Connectivity
    A visual journey from four-dimensional resting-state fMRI measurements through preprocessing, brain parcellation, regional signals, functional-connectivity graphs, machine-learning probabilities, and leakage-aware evaluation.
  • A Visual Guide to Quantization
    An interactive introduction to quantization, progressing from rounding and discrete numerical levels to bit-depth, quantization error, matrix quantization, and modern neural-network and LLM quantization.
  • Excel Function Simulator
    An interactive Excel-like spreadsheet for learning formulas by doing, with visual formula tracing, 82 supported functions across major Excel categories, and 100 guided practice exercises from beginner to advanced.
  • Mapping Metro Vancouver Transit Routes with QGIS
    A reproducible GTFS → Python → QGIS → Leaflet workflow for reconstructing, validating, styling, and publishing Metro Vancouver transit routes.
  • A Visual Guide to Randomized Controlled Trials
    An interactive visual guide to randomization, potential outcomes, treatment effects, statistical inference, power, noncompliance, attrition, advanced trial designs, and validity.
  • Nikiforov's Blow-Up Theorem: A Computational Demonstration
    A computational introduction to how abundant copies of a fixed graph force balanced blow-up structure, illustrated with triangles, planted examples, logarithmic scaling, and random-graph intuition.
  • Interactive Random Graph Generation
    This page allows users to specify the number of nodes and edges in a certain graph, and displays the properties of the generated graph after edges are added without replacement.
  • Zero-Knowledge Proofs: Several Tests in Python
    Since the landmark work on Zero-Knowldge Proof (ZKP) by Micali, Goldwasser and Rackoff, there has been tremendous progress on both theoretical and applied aspects of ZKP. In this page, I attempt to perform several experiments on multiple examples in Python, thereby demonstrating practical usefulness of this idea, especially in cryptography.
  • Testing the Limits of the nauty Package for Checking Ramsey Number (5,5)
    Computational experiments on graph generation, pruning strategies, non-isomorphic reduction, and feasibility questions arising in the search around the lower bound for \(R(5,5)\).
  • Preliminary Tests into the Reed–Solomon Proximity Gap Conjecture
    An exploratory study intended to give a concrete flavor of why the Reed–Solomon proximity gap question remains unresolved, and why it is difficult to test computationally once one approaches the Johnson-radius regime and beyond.
  • Animations for Some Basic Graphs
    Visualization of graphs is key to understanding their behavior. I vibecoded in Python to notice some basic graphs, including animations where possible.
  • Math Behind Graph Automorphism: A Demonstration
    Graph automorphism is related to graph isomorphism problem. In this page, I give a concrete exam to explain the math behind graph automorphism.
  • Devnagari OCR Using Tesseract in Linux
    Optical Character Recognition (OCR) is widely deployed tool. This is an attempt to understand how these tools perform in Devnagari scripts, using Nepali language source as a test. It is an attempt to also understand if a singular PDF file can be converted into a single editable word document.
  • Plantri in Linux for Exploring Four-color Theorem
    Four-colour theorem is a simple to understand but difficult to prove theorem that basically says that any map with any kind and number of borders can be colored by four colors such that no partion of the map is adjacent to another partion having the same color. Here, I attempt to use Plantri, a tool that generates planar graphs. A map with various partitions is effectively a planer graph, with each partition a node and each adjacency an edge.
  • Computational Burden of Graph Generation as Number of Nodes Increase
    Even if we keep a single copy of an isomorphic class, the number of graphs (all non-isomorphic) increases rapidly that even supercomputers may become a joke at some point. This is an attempt at such demonstration.
  • Black–Scholes Option Pricing: Theory and Empirical Validation
    A visual and mathematical exploration of Black–Scholes option pricing, including stochastic processes, model derivation, the Greeks, and empirical tests using S&P 500 index and Bitcoin options.
  • Computationally Replicating Four Theorems from Erdos-Renyi 1959 Paper on Random Graphs
    I have long strugged to understand the implications of the four theorems derived in Erdos-Renyi (1959) paper on Random graphs. Along with a 1956 paper by Gilbert, these two papers are strinkingly close and are said to have started research on random graph. In this page, I try to computatinally replicate four theorems from 1959 paper.
  • Harmonized System, Tariffs and Trade
    A multi-page guide to HS classification, Canadian tariff structure, preferential tariffs, Alberta trade, and applied trade-data analysis.
Graph Theory
  • Erdos-Renyi (1960): Evolution of Random Graphs in Five Phases
    This paper is a pioneer in studying evolution of random graphs from isolated graphs to completely connected graphs. I aim to computationally simulate the theoretical properties studied in this paper.
  • Directed k-Trees in Digraphs: Mathematical Foundations and Empirical Demonstration
    An interactive exploration of directed k-trees in digraphs, combining original mathematical research, rigorous derivations, graph-theoretic visualizations, and computational experiments to examine and validate key theoretical results.
  • Empirically Validating Evolution of Giant Components in Random Graphs
    A computational study of the emergence and growth of giant components in random graphs, with attention to simulation, repeated sampling, and comparison with theoretical limiting behavior.
  • Empirically Validating Evolution of First Cycles in Random Graphs
    A computational study of the emergence of different kinds of cycles in an evolving graph as studied by Knuth et. al. (1989). Theretical properties from the paper are simulated empirically.
  • Hypergraphs: From Intuitive Visualizations to Combinatorial Theorems
    An interactive exploration of hypergraphs, beginning with simple set-based representations and progressing through incidence structures, uniform hypergraphs, duality, connectivity, colouring, optimization, and major combinatorial theorems. Mathematical derivations, animated figures, and computational experiments connect intuitive examples with rigorous graph-theoretic concepts.
Mathematics
  • Error Estimation of Taylor Series Expansion
    Taylor and Maclaurin series are an important device to approximate a function, but error could be minimized by including as many terms as possible. I provide some ideas about the error estimation.
  • Discretizing Balls from 2D to 7D
    A visual treatment of radial shells, hyperspherical coordinates, angular grids, and the computational growth of discrete design spaces from the disk to the 7-dimensional ball.
