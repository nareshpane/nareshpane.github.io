One product, increasing specificity

From a chapter to a classification

The sequence below uses one arbitrary commodity example to show how classification becomes more specific as digits are added. Chapter 06 is simply the example chosen here. HS2 chapters, HS4 headings and HS6 subheadings belong to the internationally harmonized HS structure; Canada uses those same first six digits, then adds national extensions.

Demonstration Example

Assume we want to understand the classification 0601.10.11.00. Chapter 06 covers live plants and related goods; heading 06.01 narrows the classification to bulbs, tubers and related products; 0601.10 identifies dormant bulbs and related goods. The Canadian tariff item 0601.10.11 further specifies certain narcissus bulbs, while 00 is the Canadian statistical suffix. Dots are included for readability; leading zeros remain part of the classification code.

  1. 06HS2 / Chapter
  2. 06.01HS4 / Heading
  3. 0601.10HS6 / International Subheading
  4. 0601.10.11Canadian 8-digit Tariff Item

INTERNATIONALLY HARMONIZED06 + 01 + 10First 6 digits
CANADIAN EXTENSIONS11 + 00Digits 7–8 = Canadian tariff item
Digits 9–10 = Canadian statistical suffix

Other countries may attach different national digits beyond HS6. The first six digits provide the strongest direct international comparison: a U.S. 8- or 10-digit tariff line must not automatically be read as the corresponding Canadian 8- or 10-digit line.

    Where the international hierarchy ends

    Beyond HS6 in Canada

    HS6 is the international subheading. HS8 is the Canadian tariff item. HS10 is the Canadian statistical classification, including the two-digit statistical suffix.

    For 0601.10.11.00, read 0601.10 as the international subheading, add 11 for the tariff item, then 00 for the statistical suffix.

    A six-digit code alone does not specify every Canadian tariff rate or reporting detail. The applicable treatment also depends on the full tariff item and the relevant conditions, including origin.

    Open “Canadian tariff details” beneath an HS6 row for the Canadian tariff-item and statistical rows in this snapshot. The official schedules in References & Sources provide legal notes and complete schedules.

    Reading the official schedule

    What the CBSA table columns mean

    Tariff Item
    The code column includes four- and six-digit hierarchy rows and eight-digit Canadian tariff items. Its label does not mean that every displayed row is eight digits.
    SS
    The two-digit statistical suffix. Combine it with an eight-digit tariff item to form the ten-digit classification.
    Description of Goods
    The product wording and its nested qualifiers. Read it with the parent descriptions and applicable section and chapter notes.
    Unit of Meas.
    The unit specified for statistical quantity reporting. For example, NMB means number; KGM means kilograms.
    MFN Tariff
    The Most-Favoured-Nation tariff rate for goods entitled to that treatment. A rate may be “Free”, a percentage, a specific duty or a combination.
    Applicable Preferential Tariffs
    Rates for listed preferential tariff treatments. Eligibility depends on the treatment’s conditions; an acronym alone does not establish entitlement.

    Canadian national provisions

    Canadian Special Classification Chapters

    Chapters 98 and 99 contain Canadian national provisions. They are shown separately here and are not treated as ordinary international HS chapters or added to Section XXI.

    Chapter 77 is different: it is reserved for possible future use in the Harmonized System and remains in Section XV with no classification children.

    How the source data were collected

    This is a reproducible snapshot of the CBSA HTML schedules, not a separately certified international HS nomenclature. Codes, descriptions and source anomalies are retained so readers can trace the data back to CBSA.

    1. HTML → structured hierarchy. The Python program reads section headings and chapter links from the master page, then selects tariff tables by their six column labels. It separates the code, SS and description cells; skips repeated table headers; preserves leading zeros; and distinguishes HS4, HS6 and Canadian tariff-item rows.
    2. Optional PDFs → local reference. The same master page supplies PDF links. The optional downloader saves them locally; References & Sources links to CBSA’s documents.

    Repeated parent descriptions are removed only when they exactly match the heading prefix. Intermediate commodity groups remain in the description. When no HS6 row exists, an unsplit eight-digit .00 tariff item can supply the six-digit prefix and its description. Some unsplit rows also carry the heading. Every reconstructed node records its provenance. Each HS6 also retains all associated eight-digit Canadian tariff-item rows and their statistical suffixes, with the description, unit, MFN tariff and preferential tariff text from each source row. Only whitespace is normalized; blank cells and differing rates remain separate. These are national tariff-line attributes, not attributes of the international HS6.

    If only statistical rows exist, the parser retains their common description path and flags the node for review. It stops rather than guesses when no safe common path is available. In particular, the Chapter 71 HTML contains statistical-only prefixes and literal “null” text; raw descriptions for those rows are available inside the flagged HS6 nodes.

      Warnings report source ambiguities; passing structural checks does not resolve them. Chapter 6 regression checks verify 06 → 0601 → 0601.10 and inference of 0602.10 from 0602.10.00. Chapter 15 checks verify the lard line 1501.10.00 00, unit KGM, MFN Free and its full preferential-tariff text.       Discover the source links

      def discover(master, master_url):
    """Use section headers, chapter rows, and their actual HTML/PDF links."""
    sections, special = [], []
    for table in master.select('main table'):
        header = table.find_previous('h3')
        match = re.match(r'Section ([IVX]+):\s*(.*)', clean(header.get_text(' '))) if header else None
        if not match:
            continue
        section = {'code': match[1], 'description': match[2], 'chapters': []}
        for row in table.select('tr'):
            cells = row.find_all(['th', 'td'], recursive=False)
            if len(cells) != 3:
                continue
            label = BeautifulSoup(str(cells[0]), 'lxml')
            for extra in label.select('ol, ul'):
                extra.decompose()  # subchapter summaries are not chapter titles
            chapter_match = re.match(r'Chapter (\d+)\s*[-–]\s*(.*)', clean(label.get_text(' ')))
            if not chapter_match:
                continue
            code = chapter_match[1].zfill(2)
            links = {clean(a.get_text(' ')): urljoin(master_url, a['href'])
                     for a in cells[2].select('a[href]')}
            html_url = links.get('HTML')
            if not html_url:
                raise ValueError(f'No chapter HTML link for {code}')
            chapter = {'code': code, 'display_code': code,
                       'description': chapter_match[2], 'source_url': html_url,
                       'pdf_url': next((u for u in links.values() if urlparse(u).path.endswith('.pdf')), None),
                       'effective_date': clean(cells[1].get_text()),
                       'reserved': code == '77', 'section_code': section['code'],
                       'headings': []}
            if code in {'98', '99'}:
                chapter['section_code'] = None
                chapter['national_provision'] = True
                special.append(chapter)
            else:
                section['chapters'].append(chapter)
        if section['chapters']:
            sections.append(section)
    pdf_links = sorted({urljoin(master_url, a['href']) for a in master.select('main a[href]')
                        if urlparse(urljoin(master_url, a['href'])).path.lower().endswith('.pdf')})
    return sections, special, pdf_links

      Reconstruct the HS hierarchy

      def parse_chapter(chapter, soup):
    headings, explicit, candidates = {}, {}, {}
    tariff_lines = {}
    current = None
    example = None
    for values in tariff_rows(soup):
        display, suffix, description, unit, mfn, preferences = values
        if not display:
            continue  # unnumbered explanatory/group rows are not HS nodes
        if re.fullmatch(r'\d{2}\.\d{2}', display):
            code = display.replace('.', '')
            if code in headings:
                raise ValueError(f'Duplicate HS4 row: {code}')
            current = node(code, description, chapter['source_url'],
                           chapter_code=chapter['code'], extraction='explicit_hs4_row', subheadings=[])
            headings[code] = current
        elif re.fullmatch(r'\d{4}\.\d{2}(?:\.\d{2})?', display):
            code = display.replace('.', '')
            if current is None or current['code'] != code[:4]:
                if code[:4] in headings:
                    raise ValueError(f'Out-of-order HS4 parent for {display}')
                # Unsplit headings (e.g. 0205.00.00) can omit HS4 as well.
                # The flattened description starts with the heading before
                # CBSA's space-hyphen-space hierarchy separators.
                current = node(code[:4], description.split(' - ')[0], chapter['source_url'],
                               chapter_code=chapter['code'], extraction='inferred_from_child_row',
                               inferred_from=display, subheadings=[])
                headings[code[:4]] = current
            hs6 = code[:6]  # strings throughout: preserve leading zeros
            if len(code) == 6:
                if hs6 in explicit:
                    raise ValueError(f'Duplicate HS6 row: {display}')
                explicit[hs6] = description
            else:
                # SS distinguishes statistical detail from the base tariff item.
                candidates.setdefault(hs6, []).append((code, suffix, description))
                # Retain every Canadian source row, including blank-SS base
                # rows and each statistical suffix. Never inherit or merge rates.
                tariff_lines.setdefault(hs6, []).append(dict(zip(
                    ['tariff_item', 'statistical_suffix', 'description', 'unit',
                     'mfn_tariff', 'preferential_tariffs'], values)))
                if display == '0601.10.11' and suffix == '00':
                    example = dict(zip(['tariff_item', 'ss', 'description', 'unit', 'mfn', 'preferences'], values))
        else:
            raise ValueError(f'Unrecognized tariff code cell: {display!r}')
    for hs6 in sorted(set(explicit) | set(candidates)):
        parent = headings[hs6[:4]]
        inferred = hs6 not in explicit
        evidence = []
        source_warning = None
        if not inferred:
            full = explicit[hs6]
        else:
            rows = candidates[hs6]
            evidence = sorted({r[0] for r in rows})
            # An unsplit .00 tariff item carries the international description.
            # Prefer the blank-SS base row over more detailed statistical rows.
            base_rows = [r for r in rows if r[0].endswith('00') and r[1] in {'', '00'}]
            if not base_rows:
                if not all(r[0].endswith('00') for r in rows):
                    raise ValueError(f'Cannot safely infer HS6 description for {hs6}; inspect source')
                # Some source rows have only statistical suffixes and literal
                # "null" text. Retain the common ancestor path, flag it visibly,
                # and retain all verbatim descriptions for manual verification.
                paths = [r[2].split(' - ') for r in rows]
                common = []
                for parts in zip(*paths):
                    if len(set(parts)) != 1 or parts[0] == 'null':
                        break
                    common.append(parts[0])
                if len(common) < 2:
                    raise ValueError(f'No common HS6 description path: {hs6}')
                full = ' - '.join(common)
                source_warning = ('Source HTML has only statistical rows for this prefix, '
                                  'with no base tariff-item row. The common description path '
                                  'is retained; verify classification against the official schedule.')
            else:
                full = min((r[2] for r in base_rows), key=len)
        parent['subheadings'].append(node(
            hs6, relative_description(full, parent['description']), chapter['source_url'],
            heading_code=parent['code'], chapter_code=chapter['code'],
            source_description=full,
            extraction='inferred_from_tariff_item' if inferred else 'explicit_hs6_row',
            inferred_from=evidence, source_warning=source_warning,
            source_row_descriptions=[r[2] for r in candidates.get(hs6, [])] if source_warning else [],
            canadian_tariff_lines=tariff_lines.get(hs6, [])))
    chapter['headings'] = list(headings.values())
    if not chapter['reserved'] and not headings:
        raise ValueError(f'No tariff headings parsed for chapter {chapter["code"]}')
    return example

      Download optional reference PDFs

      def download_pdfs(fetcher, pdf_links):
    downloaded = 0
    for index, url in enumerate(pdf_links, 1):
        # URLs come from anchors in the master page, never a hand-written list.
        destination = BASE / 'source-pdfs' / Path(urlparse(url).path).name
        _, fetched = fetcher.get(url, destination, pdf=True)
        downloaded += int(fetched)
        print(f'PDF {index}/{len(pdf_links)}: {destination.name} ({"downloaded" if fetched else "cached"})', flush=True)
    return downloaded

        How the Section 338 lens is constructed
        1. Read U.S. product codes from the CSV’s hts8 column. ch99_heading identifies the U.S. measure, not the product code.
        2. Remove formatting punctuation and spaces, preserve leading zeros, and validate eight digits. Unexpected or reserved/national product codes stop generation for review.
        3. Extract each unique six-digit HS prefix and match it to Canada’s HS6 hierarchy. Retain its HS4 and HS2 ancestors.
        4. Show Canadian tariff-item and statistical descendants beneath matched HS6 codes for context. No equivalence between Canadian and U.S. national extensions is claimed.

        Unmatched HS6 values and their U.S. source codes are retained in the lookup and reported during generation; runtime matching also reports unmatched prefixes.
