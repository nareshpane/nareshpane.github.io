    A code is the starting point

    Where preferential treatment begins

    MFN is the baseline treatment shown in Canada’s tariff schedule. Preferential programs and trade agreements can offer a different treatment for eligible goods. Abbreviations such as UST, MXT, CEUT and CPTPT identify treatments; an HS6 code alone cannot establish eligibility.

    Tariff item→Origin & agreement→Rules & evidence→Applicable treatment

    Read the CBSA country-treatment list and administrative guidelines alongside the full tariff item. Page 1 explains the hierarchy.

    Methodology, reconciliation & limitations

    One year, one destination

    The local Statistics Canada CIMT domestic-export archive supplies monthly customs-based observations. The builder selects calendar year 2025 and Country/Pays = US, then sums all twelve months and U.S. states. Province is the source’s origin attribution. Domestic exports exclude re-exports and describe Canadian production-linked trade.

    The schema selects ODPFN018_202512N.csv: HS6, origin, destination, year-month and value fields. Adjacent 016 and 020 files contain HS8 and HS2 and are not stacked into this dataset. Values are integer CAD; quantities and units are not aggregated across unlike products.

    Canada is a construction

    The source has thirteen origins and no separate Canada-origin record. Every displayed product uses their sum, with exact integer assertions at HS6 and HS4 and for each province total. Codes remain strings, including leading zeroes; matching removes punctuation. HS4 uses the first four digits and HS6 the first six.

    Canada exports for product h = Σ13 origins origin exports for h
    Province share = province exports for productCanada exports for product × 100

    In the drilldown, “Share within province” divides each child’s exports by that province’s exports of the selected HS4 heading. A zero denominator is unavailable. The heatmap uses a square-root sequential scale to keep smaller flows visible; cells retain readable values.

    Descriptions and Canadian tariff context

    HS6 descriptions use ODPF_4_HS6XDesc.TXT, restricted to validity dates overlapping 2025. Country and province names use their date-aware fixed-width lookups. HS4 descriptions reuse Page 1’s CBSA hierarchy: explicit headings, or common heading wording confirmed across all children. No arbitrary HS6 child supplies an HS4 description. Missing compatible descriptions are labelled.

    Exact prefixes connect the 2025 exports to the separate 2026 CBSA import snapshot. National provisions 980200 and 990100 lack compatible CBSA detail here. This is not a full classification concordance. HS6 can contain several Canadian HS8 items and statistical rows with different treatments. Original blank cells and differing rates remain separate. Full treatment depends on the HS8 item, origin, applicable rules and evidence.

    Deduplication without losing trade

    Monthly records for the same product, province and country are observations to sum. State, quantity, unit, month and every source field enter the complete-record duplicate check. The source has no exact duplicate records; none were removed. The builder stops for review if repeats appear in a future source. Raw, filtered, annual, province and reconstructed Canada totals remain in the audit.

    Section 338 reconstruction

    The supplied CSVs have identical SHA-256 hashes, columns, row counts and records. The canonical copy is s338_products_browser.csv. Its July list supplies product lines, programs and Chapter 99 headings, not reliable rates, dates or current status.

    September Annex II texts replace HTS8 parents with retained HTS10 children and add products; Annex I additions and removals cross-check the changes. Unrestricted ban lines are excluded next. Packaged-only bans retain the tariff line because other goods can remain covered. Amendments occur at the detailed U.S. line level before taking unique HS6 prefixes. A set membership match cannot multiply Canadian observations.

    The local ban annexes do not establish September 29 effective dates themselves. This is a reconstruction of supplied September material, not a certified current legal schedule. The files do not establish a Yale Budget Lab provenance chain, so no Yale authorship is assumed.

    • Values are domestic exports, not total exports including re-exports; province refers to StatCan origin attribution.
    • Canada is reconstructed from thirteen origins; monthly observations become annual 2025 totals.
    • HS4 and HS6 are derived from the first four and six digits respectively.
    • Canadian HS8 and U.S. HTS8/HTS10 classifications are not assumed identical.
    • HS6 matching approximates product scope; it does not mean every Canadian HS8 item or shipment is covered.
    • Matched exports are neither duties paid nor tariff revenue, economic loss or projected export reduction.
    • Preferential entitlement cannot be determined from HS6 alone. Full treatment depends on HS8, origin and applicable rules.
    • Packaged-only restrictions and shipment exceptions cannot be resolved from aggregated exports; subsequent source revisions may differ.
