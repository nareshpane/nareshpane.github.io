07.1 / Rights on a digital underlying

Bitcoin Options and Their Economic Purpose

A Bitcoin option separates the right to a future payoff from ownership of the asset today. A holder can limit the initial option outlay while taking exposure to price changes or uncertainty. A put can hedge a BTC holding; a call can express upside exposure. Neither position removes counterparty, liquidity or opportunity-cost risk.

For an illustrative $10,000 strike, a call has positive intrinsic value when the expiration reference price exceeds $10,000; a put has positive intrinsic value below it. The premium is paid before the outcome is known. Exercise at expiry, resale before expiry and profit after costs are different concepts.

07.2 / One claim, two units of account

How Deribit Bitcoin Options Work

Deribit’s 2019 explanation documents European exercise, automatic cash settlement and the BTC–date–strike–side identifier. Its January 2020 announcement identifies 08:00 UTC expiry and the BTC index. A 2020 call-options explanation explicitly converts dollar intrinsic value into BTC at settlement.

Those sources support the historical mechanics, not every specification of an uninspected record. The current specification states a one-BTC multiplier and describes a settlement-process change from August 2026. That later futures-intermediated process must not be retrofitted onto the earlier dataset. Historical multiplier, reference-index composition, delivery-price averaging and fee schedules still require matching to actual observation dates. Prices here exclude fees, funding and collateral effects.

Terminal payoff · one BTC of notional
\[H_T^{\mathrm{BTC},C}=\frac{(S_T-K)^+}{S_T},\qquad H_T^{\mathrm{BTC},P}=\frac{(K-S_T)^+}{S_T}.\]

Here \(S_T\) is the terminal conversion reference in USD/BTC and \(K\) is the dollar strike. Multiplying either BTC payoff by \(S_T\) gives its dollar-equivalent intrinsic value. Quantity and a verified contract multiplier scale the payment separately.

Why USD value divided by spot is a benchmark, not a universal inverse-option formula

Choose the USD money-market account as numeraire. If frictionless conversion at the terminal index is feasible, the BTC payment \(H_T^{\rm BTC}\) has terminal dollar value \(S_TH_T^{\rm BTC}\). For the payoff above this equals the conventional dollar intrinsic value. With a tradable spot, the stated financing assumptions and no basis or delivery frictions, its dollar value can therefore be modeled by a European formula; expressing that value in BTC now divides by \(S_t\).

\[V_t^{\rm USD}=e^{-r(T-t)}\mathbb E^{\mathbb Q_{\rm USD}}[S_TH_T^{\rm BTC}\mid\mathcal F_t],\qquad V_t^{\rm BTC}=V_t^{\rm USD}/S_t.\]

\(\mathcal F_t\) denotes information available at time t. This is not the USD-risk-neutral expectation of the BTC payoff alone. In this expectation T denotes the expiration clock time; the calculator below uses T for the remaining year fraction. A BTC-numeraire expectation uses a different measure and financing asset. A nontradable delivery index, forward basis, lending yield, exchange collateral and fees can prevent the simple replication argument from representing the actual contract exactly.

07.4 / Independent inputs, explicit assumptions

Estimating the Model Inputs

Actual valuation spot: each record’s index_price. The separate history is an arithmetic mean of available Bitstamp, Bitfinex, Coinbase, itBit and Kraken prices; all 2,697,313 supplied averages reconcile. Despite its filename, Price_5m.csv has exact one-minute spacing from January 1, 2015 08:02 to February 17, 2020 11:14. Constituent availability changes over time; this proxy is not silently relabelled the Deribit index.

The history clock has no encoded timezone. UTC is the declared interpretation: a stratified cross-date check against epoch-anchored trade indices gives a median absolute log gap of 5 bp, versus 111 bp under UTC+8. This supports alignment, not independent proof of the original clock convention. A minute labelled t is treated as available only at t + 60 seconds; joins are backward and at most five minutes old. Daily 23:59 closes become available at the next UTC midnight. Individual updates of the co-recorded index are not timestamped separately.

Rates: use the dataset’s daily irate, an annual fractional T-bill proxy identified by the paper as Yahoo ^IRX, available here only from next UTC midnight. The approximation is r = 2 log(1 + y/2), not an exact discount-yield conversion or zero-curve bootstrap. FRED access failed in this environment; these values are not labelled DGS3MO. No 2026 rate is used. USD financing, BTC funding, basis and collateral remain omitted from q = 0.

\[u_d=\log(S_d/S_{d-1}),\qquad H_W=\sqrt{365}\sqrt{\frac{1}{W-1}\sum_{j=1}^{W}(u_j-\bar u)^2},\quad W\in\{30,60\}.\]

A completed UTC day’s last recorded price becomes usable as that day’s close at the next midnight. Each estimate needs W consecutive valid daily returns, hence W + 1 closing prices. No forward filling bridges missing days, and the trade day’s future close cannot enter its estimate. This is a calendar-day convention, not the equity √252 convention.

Optional five-minute realized volatility and historical rates

The optional estimator requires an exact five-minute UTC grid, 288 returns in each complete day and W complete days. It uses quadratic variation, \(\sqrt{(365/W)\sum u_{5m}^2}\), without interpolation. This differs from the centered daily sample standard deviation; microstructure noise, overnight conventions and sampling coverage need their own checks.

DGS3MO is a candidate USD rate proxy, not a 2026 input for old transactions. The adapter requires rate-availability timestamps or a separately justified availability assumption. Missing yields stay missing. The conversion \(r=2\log(1+y/2)\), with y decimal, is an approximate semiannual-equivalent transformation, not an exact Treasury zero curve. Sensitivities include zero rate and ±50 basis points; these do not substitute for a verified historical source.

07.5 / A common evaluation set

Observed versus Theoretical Option Prices

Experiment A · Independent historical volatility

Calculate European BSM values with H30 and H60, a verified contemporaneous underlying and an available historical rate. Convert a recorded BTC premium with the same documented USD/BTC basis. Error = theoretical value − observed premium. A trade price is not a quote midpoint, and an exchange mark is not an independent executed premium.

Experiment B · Earlier training observations

The executed baseline fits one common σ on a period strictly before a declared UTC cutoff, then holds that σ fixed for later evaluation. Training needs at least twenty trades and three contracts; σ is bounded between 1% and 400%. This parsimonious benchmark may be stale across regimes. A future maturity-specific extension would require a separately documented design.

All principal comparisons use the same held-out rows with valid H30, H60, rates and source inputs. Initial research restrictions are 7–90 days and 0.80 ≤ K/S ≤ 1.20. They precede residual inspection; large errors alone are not an exclusion rule. The code reports every filtering stage, backward-match lags, actual trade-ID duplicates when available and side/date/contract counts. It does not invent displayed sizes, bid/ask fields or spread filters for transaction-only data.

Paired improvement
\[\Delta_i=|V_i^{H}-p_i|-|V_i^{B}-p_i|.\]

Positive Δ means calibration improved absolute fit for that observation. Report its mean, median and share positive; separately aggregate per contract when contracts repeat. Percentage reduction in aggregate MAE is \(100(\mathrm{MAE}_H-\mathrm{MAE}_B)/\mathrm{MAE}_H\), not an average of transaction-level percentage improvements.

Computed metrics include MAE, median absolute error, RMSE, signed/median bias, spot-normalized basis points, BTC errors and explicit tolerances. Premium-relative errors exclude observations below a disclosed $5 floor and report their denominator count. Spread coverage exists only for independently supplied, synchronized bid/ask fields. 07.6 / Inversion is description, not validation

Volatility Smile, Moneyness, and Maturity

Experiment C independently inverts valid recorded premiums using a bracketed solver. A good re-pricing fit after inversion is a numerical identity, not new evidence of pricing accuracy. Market-implied σ is a price summary; H30 and H60 are historical estimates. Neither comparison alone establishes how well future volatility is forecast.

Independent inversion succeeds for 177,977 of 178,236 held-out premiums; 259 fail model bounds or the [0,500%] volatility bracket. Those failures remain in the primary pricing-error sample. Median market IV is 66%, versus 59% H30 and 63% H60. Vendor IV is a separate percentage field; among 177,975 joint valid values, the mean absolute difference from our inversion is 0.4 basis points. Agreement of inversions checks numerical conventions, not independent empirical accuracy.

07.7 / What is established now?

What the Bitcoin Evidence Establishes

What the data show: H60 fits this held-out period better than H30 in both MAE and RMSE. Both historical benchmarks underprice on average. The earlier fitted constant σ overprices on average and performs markedly worse; fitting an earlier market regime is not a guarantee of future-date pricing accuracy. These results do not show that every volatility calibration fails, or that H60 is universally optimal.

Positive Delta means calibration reduced absolute error. Negative mean differences and negative aggregate-MAE reductions here indicate worse calibrated fit. The paired table responds to the same side/maturity controls as the pricing figures.

Executed trade prices are not bid/ask midpoints. There are no quotes or spreads in these files, so within-spread coverage and executable arbitrage are not inferred. Index-update timing, exchange-average composition, BTC financing, jumps, changing volatility, fees and collateral can matter, but this study does not identify a causal share for each factor. Numerical correctness, empirical fit, future volatility forecasting and trading profitability are distinct claims.
