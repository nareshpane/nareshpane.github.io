What is Texas Hold’em?

A shared-card poker game: each player receives two private hole cards, then up to five community cards appear face up. At showdown, the strongest five-card hand wins.

KnownYour cards, the exposed board, and public betting actions.
UnknownOther players’ cards, future community cards, and opponents’ intentions.
Mathematical tensionA fixed ranking, but changing information about who will win.

Standard deck: 52 distinct cards, 13 ranks in each of four suits, no jokers. Suits have equal status: ♠ spades, ♥ hearts, ♦ diamonds, ♣ clubs.

01 · Anatomy of a deal

Information arrives in stages.

  1. Preflop2 private

    Each player receives two hole cards. No board yet.

  2. Flop+ 3 shared

    Three community cards appear together.

  3. Turn+ 1 shared

    The fourth community card is revealed.

  4. River+ 1 shared

    The fifth and final community card appears.

  5. Showdownbest 5

    Players still in the hand compare their best five.

Betting follows the deal of hole cards and each board stage. Players may check (pass when no bet is owed), bet, call (match), raise (increase), or fold (give up the hand), as the action permits.

Two ways to win. Have the best hand at showdown, or be the last player remaining after everyone else folds. The pot is the chips contributed to the hand; compulsory small and big blinds start it. A hand can end before all five community cards are dealt.

Ten familiar names; nine general categories. A royal flush is the ace-high straight flush, not a separate mathematical class. In counting problems, include it among straight flushes unless explicitly separating it. Always assign a hand its highest category: a full house contains a pair, but is classified as a full house.

05 · Within the same category

The first difference decides.

Compare the defining ranks first, then any remaining kickers in descending order. A kicker is a card outside the main repeated-rank group that can settle an otherwise tied hand. Ace normally ranks above king; suits never break a showdown tie.

07 · The next mathematical question

Why are some hands worth more than others?

Matching ranks, consecutive ranks, and matching suits impose different constraints. Some patterns occupy far fewer possibilities in a deck than others. The standard hierarchy follows the rarity of the exclusive categories in a uniformly chosen five-card hand.

♠ ♥
♦ ♣52 distinct cards
C(52, 5)unordered five-card hands

“Unordered” means that dealing the same five cards in a different order does not create a new hand. Next comes the counting: how many of these hands realize each pattern?

Keep the experiment precise. Choosing five cards from a deck and choosing the best five from seven are different experiments. The fixed hand ranking stays the same in Hold’em; it is not recalculated from seven-card frequencies or from the current board.

C(n, k) = n!k! (n − k)!

n = number of distinct available objects; k = number selected, with 0 ≤ k ≤ n. The factorial n! means n × (n − 1) × … × 1, and 0! = 1. C(n, k), also written “n choose k,” counts unordered selections without replacement.

7 × 6 × 5 × 4 × 3Count choices for five successive positions.
÷ 5! = ÷ 120Each selected set appears in 120 different orders.
= C(7, 5) = 21Exactly 21 distinct five-card subsets.

Why the factorial formula? The ordered count is n! / (n − k)!; dividing by the k! arrangements of each selected set removes duplicate orderings. For seven cards: C(7, 5) = 7! / (5! 2!) = 21.

09 · Define the sample space

How many five-card hands are possible?

Assume a thoroughly shuffled standard deck: every set of five distinct cards is equally likely. The sample space Ω is the collection of these sets; |Ω| is its size.

One royal flush, two written orders
A♠♠A♠K♠♠K♠Q♠♠Q♠J♠♠J♠10♠♠10♠
= same five physical cards
10♠♠10♠J♠♠J♠Q♠♠Q♠K♠♠K♠A♠♠A♠

|Ω| = C(52, 5)= 52 × 51 × 50 × 49 × 485 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1= 311,875,200120 = 2,598,960

The numerator counts ordered deals. For every particular five-card set, there are 5! = 120 orders, so divide once by 120. A shuffled deck has no repeated physical cards, even when two cards share a rank.

P(category) = number of hands in that category2,598,960 equally likely hands

P denotes probability. For example, the four royal flushes have probability 4 / 2,598,960 = 1 / 649,740 ≈ 0.000154%. This concerns an ordinary five-card deal.

Do not multiply a five-card probability by 21. The candidate subsets overlap heavily. Computing seven-card frequencies requires counting seven-card sets and classifying each by its best subset.

14 · Conditioning changes the denominator

“Given what I know” is part of the probability.

P(A | B) = P(A ∩ B)P(B), with P(B) > 0

A is the event of interest; B is the given information expressed as an event. The vertical bar means “given”; A ∩ B means both events happen. Conditioning restricts the sample space to B and rescales its probability to 1.

52 candidatesA: candidate card is a heart.13 hearts

Remove 5 knownA♥ Q♥ 8♥ 3♥ K♣4 hearts + 1 non-heart

47 candidates remainB: candidate is not a known card.9 hearts

To model card removal, start with a uniformly selected candidate from all 52 cards and condition on it being outside the fixed known set. Before that restriction, P(A ∩ B) = 9/52 and P(B) = 47/52.

P(A | B) = (9/52) / (47/52) = 9/47 ≈ 19.149%

The original 13/52 = 25% heart fraction is no longer the right denominator-and-numerator pair. Four hearts are already visible, so the next unseen card is a heart with probability 9/47. The deck’s physical size stays 52; your set of possible next cards shrinks.

16 · Inference from an observation

Bayes’ rule: weight possibilities by their predictions.

Use a deliberately simplified, hypothetical opponent model. At one fixed decision point, every possible holding belongs to exactly one of two classes: H (“strong”) or L (“other”). These are model labels, not specific poker categories.

Prior model · before the action

P(H | B) = P(B | H) P(H)P(B | H) P(H) + P(B | L) P(L)= 0.75 × 0.200.75 × 0.20 + 0.25 × 0.80= 0.15 / 0.35 = 3/7 ≈ 42.857%

P(H) and P(L) are prior probabilities, summing to 1. P(B | H) and P(B | L) are the assumed probabilities of the observed action in each class. The denominator is P(B), the total probability of a bet; P(H | B) is the updated probability of H.

In words: posterior ∝ likelihood × prior. The symbol ∝ means “proportional to”; divide both class weights by their sum to make probabilities add to one. H becomes more plausible after a bet, but L still accounts for more betting cases because it was much more common initially.

Assumptions in, conclusions out. The class proportions and action policy above are invented teaching assumptions, not measured player behavior or a strategy recommendation. Different priors or policies give different posteriors. This example demonstrates inference, not mind-reading.
17 · Bridge to decision mathematics

A likely outcome is not the same as an average payoff.

Probability describes possible outcomes. Expected value weights the net payoff of each outcome by its probability.

EV = Σi pi xi

i indexes mutually exclusive, exhaustive outcomes. pi is the probability of outcome i, with Σi pi = 1; xi is its signed net payoff measured from the same starting point. Σ means add all outcomes’ contributions.

Win · probability 3/10+20 units netContribution: (3/10)(20) = +6
Lose · probability 7/10−5 units netContribution: (7/10)(−5) = −3.5
Expected net payoff+2.5 unitsEV = 6 − 3.5 = 2.5

In this hypothetical two-outcome example there are no ties or additional costs. A single play returns either +20 or −5, never +2.5. EV is the probability-weighted mean; positive EV still comes with a 70% chance of losing on one play.

Keep the meanings separate. A draw-completion probability is not automatically a win probability. To evaluate an actual decision, the outcomes, net payoffs, ties, future actions, and uncertainty about other players must be modeled consistently.

21 · Sampling uncertainty

A sample estimate has a scale of fluctuation.

Code each trial as 1 for a target hit and 0 for a miss: a Bernoulli outcome. Under the independent-trial model, the standard error is the standard deviation of p̂ across repeated samples of size n.

SE(p̂) = √[p(1 − p)/n]Estimated SE(p̂) ≈ √[p̂(1 − p̂)/n]

p is the true event probability; p̂ is its sample estimate; n is the number of independent trials. The first expression is the theoretical standard error. The second substitutes p̂ when p is unknown. Both use probability units; multiply by 100 for percentage points (pp).

For the exact flush probability p = 378/1,081, each interval below spans p ± one theoretical SE. These are illustrations of sampling scale, not guaranteed bounds or 95% confidence intervals.

22 · Two computational approaches

List the possibilities, or sample them.

Exact enumeration

List every relevant outcome and evaluate each once. For a fixed flop and your hole cards, there are only C(47, 2) = 1,081 unordered turn–river pairs.

  • Exact and deterministic under the stated uniform model.
  • No Monte Carlo sampling error.
  • Work grows with the number of states and the cost per evaluation.

For this laboratory, full enumeration is practical and supplies the reference values.

Monte Carlo

Sample from the same outcome distribution and replace exact proportions with empirical frequencies.

  • Flexible when the outcome model becomes more complex.
  • Work can be limited by a chosen trial budget.
  • Sampling error remains; rare events may need many trials.

Useful in computational statistics for estimating probabilities and expectations when exhaustive sums are expensive.

The correct distribution matters in both methods. If outcomes are not equally likely, enumeration needs probability weights and simulation must sample from those weights. A deterministic seed supports reproducibility, not proof of accuracy.

23 · Combinatorial growth

The size depends on what a “state” records.

1,081
C(47, 2)

Unordered turn–river cards, given your hole cards and flop. Order is irrelevant to the final hand.

1,070,190
C(47, 2) × C(45, 2)

An unordered runout plus one opponent’s unordered hole pair, disjoint from the runout, given those same five known cards.

2,598,960
C(52, 5)

All unordered five-card sets from a full deck.

133,784,560
C(52, 7)

All unordered seven-card sets from a full deck; each has 21 five-card candidates.

The examples have different information assumptions; they are not successive stages of one identical sample space. In the second row, 47 cards remain initially, then 45 after selecting the runout. This counts assignments of cards, not betting histories or player strategies.

C(52, 7) = 52!/(7!45!) = 133,784,560C(47, 2) × C(45, 2) = 1,081 × 990 = 1,070,190

These finite spaces are not inherently impossible to compute. Their size, the evaluation algorithm, and the extra information represented in each state determine the work. That is why choosing a precise state description is part of the mathematics.

25 · Notation at a glance

A compact mathematical reference.

C(n, k)
Number of unordered k-element subsets of n distinct objects, without replacement.
P(A)
Probability of event A under the stated model.
P(A | B)
Probability of A conditional on information B, with P(B) > 0.
o and U
Number of distinct current outs and number of unseen cards. Here P(next fixed out) = o/U.
best_hand(S)
Maximum hand value rank(T) among five-card subsets T of the available set S.
EV
Expected signed net payoff: Σᵢ pᵢxᵢ, with outcome probabilities pᵢ and net payoffs xᵢ.
p and p*
Assumed success or win probability, depending on the stated event; p* is the terminal pot model’s break-even value C/F.
p̂ = h/n
Observed hit proportion: h successes in n simulation trials. Here n counts trials; in C(n, k), it counts available objects.
SE(p̂)
Standard deviation of the sample proportion across repeated independent samples; estimated by √[p̂(1 − p̂)/n].
P₀, B, C, R, F
Pot before the opponent bet, bet, call cost, net winning reward, and final pot. In this model C = B, R = P₀ + B, F = R + C.
26 · Reproducibility and limits

The numbers can be checked.

The exact results use exhaustive enumeration. Simulation paths use a documented seed and a fixed deck order; replaying the same scenario, seed, and trial count produces the same counts. No browser experiment runs until requested.

Verification scripts and their roles
  • verify_math.py independently enumerates all 2,598,960 five-card hands and checks the combinatorial formulas, fixed-out probabilities, Bayes example, and introductory EV.
  • verify_lab.py uses Python to enumerate all 1,081 runouts for each of five scenarios and reproduce 15 seeded 10,000-trial sequences.
  • verify_lab.cjs checks the browser engine against Python: every complete-board ranking, seeded checkpoint, exact fraction, pot example, and standard error.
  • audit_poker.py audits displayed mathematics, poker examples, page structure, relative links, and the index entry.
  • verified_math.json and verified_lab.json contain the reproducible reference results.
  • poker-engine.js is the local ranking, probability, EV, and seeded sampling implementation used by the browser and Node checks.
python3 research/math-behind-poker/verify_math.py
python3 research/math-behind-poker/verify_lab.py
node research/math-behind-poker/verify_lab.cjs
python3 research/math-behind-poker/audit_poker.py

Python uses only its standard library; Node uses only built-in modules. The optional render_math.py and render_lab.py regenerate their marked static sections, including figures. Neither is required for GitHub Pages hosting.

Sampling assumptions and implementation

Each trial samples an ordered pair of distinct cards using a seeded Mulberry32 generator and rejection when mapping a 32-bit word to a deck index. Each unordered pair has two orders, so its exact probability is 1/1,081. The Python verifier independently implements the integer arithmetic and checks the JavaScript results.

Cards are restored between trials, never between the turn and river in one trial. The engine evaluates all 21 five-card subsets, including the ace-low straight exception. Runs are capped at 10,000 trials and executed in short scheduled batches; stopping, changing the experiment, or leaving the page cancels pending work.

The probability and EV models do not infer an opponent’s strategy or promise profit. Optional typography reuses the research site’s Google Fonts with local system-font fallbacks; all cards, diagrams, equations, and controls work without an external asset service.

The essential habit: define the event, state the information, count or sample consistently, then interpret the uncertainty.
