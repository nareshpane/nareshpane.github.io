Consumer Price Index, September 2026
Released at 8:30 a.m. Eastern time in The Daily, Monday, October 19, 2026
The Consumer Price Index (CPI) rose 3.1% year over year in September, following a 3.0% increase in August. The
acceleration was led by higher prices for gasoline in September (+27.8%) compared with August (+22.8%).
Excluding gasoline, the CPI rose 2.2%, after increasing 2.4% in August.
Moderating the acceleration in the all-items CPI in September were smaller year-over-year price increases for travel
tours and groceries compared with the previous month.
The CPI increased 0.1% month over month in September. On a seasonally adjusted monthly basis, the CPI
rose 0.4%.

Energy prices increase at a faster pace
Prices for energy accelerated in September, rising 19.0% year over year following a 15.4% gain in August. Crude oil
prices increased in September, reaching their highest level since May amid ongoing uncertainty about the wars in
the Middle East, resulting in higher prices for gasoline and fuel oil.
Year over year, prices for gasoline rose at a faster pace in September (+27.8%) compared with August (+22.8%).
Moderating the increase in gasoline prices in September was the switch to the less expensive winter blend.
Similarly, prices for fuel oil and other fuels rose 57.8% year over year in September, following a 43.7% increase in
August. The price increase in September had a greater impact on consumers in Atlantic Canada, where fuel oil is
more widely used.

Grocery price growth continues to slow
Prices for food purchased from stores rose 1.7% year over year in September, after increasing 2.8% in August.
Although price levels remain high, this was the smallest yearly increase since May 2024 (+1.5%), and the second
consecutive month where grocery price growth increased at a slower pace than the all-items CPI.
Driving the year-over-year slowdown in grocery prices in September 2026 were declines in prices for fresh
vegetables (-2.9%) and sugar and confectionery (-2.7%). Prices for other food preparations (+1.8%) and fresh or
frozen beef (+8.7%) rose at a slower pace in September than in August (+2.8% and +11.0%, respectively), also
contributing to the slowdown.
Despite the year-over-year slowdown in prices for groceries, prices have increased 33.0% since September 2020.

Shelter prices increase year over year
In September, shelter prices rose 1.8% year over year, up from a 1.5% increase in August. In addition to higher
prices for fuel oil and other fuels (+57.8%), driving the acceleration was faster price growth for homeowners' home
and mortgage insurance (+4.9%) and homeowners' maintenance and repairs (+1.2%).
The homeowners' replacement cost (-1.9%) and mortgage interest cost (-0.2%) indexes fell at the same rate in
September as they did in August. Meanwhile, prices for rent rose 2.8% year over year, matching the increase in
August.
Regional highlights
Year over year, prices rose at a faster pace in five provinces in September compared with August, mainly due to
higher prices for gasoline and fuel oil.
Excluding energy, inflation was higher in Nova Scotia (+3.3%), Prince Edward Island (+2.6%), New Brunswick
(+2.6%) and Newfoundland and Labrador (+2.4%) in September, compared with the movement at the national level
(+2.1%). At the provincial level and on a year-over-year basis, rent prices rose at the fastest pace in Atlantic
Canada.

Students pay more for tuition in 2026 
Tuition fees, priced annually in September, increased 2.4% in 2026 compared with a 1.7% increase in 2025.
In 2026, students from Prince Edward Island (+4.9%) experienced the largest price increase. At the same time,
students from Newfoundland and Labrador (+0.6%) had the smallest increase, coinciding with a freeze on tuition
fees.
Tuition fees in Ontario rose 2.1% year over year in 2026, the largest increase since 2018, as the province ended its
freeze on tuition fees.
