A national inflation rate condenses millions of transactions into a monthly estimate. The path from a store receipt or a rental unit to that estimate determines which changes are visible and what can be said about their uncertainty.

Read the sampling designs side by side, test the estimators on a known finite population, and check the official series through October 2026. The Canadian and U.S. headline measures follow distinct spending populations and shelter concepts.

Local prices can move differently when regions face different housing markets, transport costs and shopping patterns. Canada publishes CPI for the provinces and three northern territorial capitals, drawing on selected collection areas and other sources. The U.S. CPI-U uses urban sampling areas that can cross state lines. Each country combines those streams of price evidence using geographically detailed expenditure weights. The geographical analysis follows those choices from the local sample to the national index.

SECTION 01

What does it mean to measure inflation?

From a receipt to an index

A household pays for groceries, gasoline, a place to live, clothing, transit, and services. A $3.00 item rising to $3.30 has a price relative of 1.10. The household’s overall price movement depends on what it bought and the dollars at stake. A national CPI combines weighted price movements across many markets. Individual prices are measured in currency, a price level is an aggregate concept, and a CPI expresses movement relative to a reference period.

Monthly inflation compares adjacent months. Twelve-month inflation compares the same month one year earlier. Index reference periods differ, so compare movements after rebasing to a shared date. A personal index can use a household’s quantities or spending shares. A theoretical cost-of-living index compares minimum expenditures needed to attain a given utility under two price schedules. The utility level \(u\) is held fixed and consumers can substitute. Operational indexes use observable prices, weights, and practical conventions to approximate a defined measurement objective. SECTION 02

Statistical populations and measurement scope

Coverage and frames

Statistics Canada’s scope chapter excludes institutional and collective households, Canadian Forces members living in military camps, expenditures of residents of First Nations reserves, and territorial locations outside Whitehorse, Yellowknife and Iqaluit. Included consumer purchases are domestic transactions with establishments operating in Canada. Direct income taxes and investments sit outside the product scope. [SC §3]

The Canadian target concerns purchases by households in Canada, including urban and rural locations in the provinces, with territorial coverage represented through the capitals where published. The private-household expenditure universe and available urban collection geographies are distinct. Institutions, collective living arrangements and activity outside operational frames need separate consideration. Canadian prices may also reflect nationally priced services or online offers serving more than the collection city. [SC scope] [SC §5]

CPI-U represents consumers in U.S. urban areas, including metropolitan and micropolitan areas in the BLS design. BLS describes coverage of approximately 93% of the population using the 2010 Census and 2018 redesign definitions. People outside that urban scope, and people in institutions and the armed forces as defined in the program, are outside the target. CPI-W uses the spending of qualifying urban wage-earner and clerical-worker households. C-CPI-U covers the CPI-U population and uses more current expenditure information in its final chained formula. Sampling paths

Probability sampling theory

For a fixed finite population \(U=\{1,\ldots,N\}\) and sample \(s\subseteq U\), let \(S_i=\mathbf 1\{i\in s\}\), \(\pi_i=E_pS_i\) and \(\pi_{ij}=E_p(S_iS_j)\). Along a unique nested path, the inclusion probability factors by the chain rule. Multiple paths that can reach the same unit require their union probability. Equal to one for certainty units, \(d_i=1/\pi_i\) is a design weight for positive inclusion probability. [Horvitz–Thompson] [CPI Manual]

Derive the design expectation and variance

Taking expectation term by term gives \(\sum_i y_i E_p(S_i)/\pi_i=\sum_i y_i\). Taking covariance between pairs gives \(E_p(S_iS_j)-E_pS_iE_pS_j=\pi_{ij}-\pi_i\pi_j\). Use \(\pi_{ii}=\pi_i\).

A Hájek mean divides the HT total by the estimated population size \(\sum_{s} d_i\). It trades exact design unbiasedness in general for a ratio that often stabilizes scale. Stratification samples separately within groups. PPS draws favor larger size measures, and the distinction between draw probabilities with replacement and distinct-unit inclusion probabilities matters. Calibration changes weights to match known auxiliary totals, with accuracy dependent on auxiliaries, overlap and the relationship with price change. These are general survey estimators. Agency production combines elementary indexes and expenditure cost weights. [Neyman] [BLS calculation] [SC §6]

SECTION 04

Randomization, representativeness and selection bias

Design-based inference treats the finite values as fixed and averages over random sample draws. Model-based inference averages under assumptions about the data or selection mechanism. Model-assisted estimation uses a model to improve estimation while retaining a probability-design justification. Where selection is informative, larger samples may concentrate around a biased limit. Where selection is independent of the outcome conditional on observed covariates, a suitable adjustment may reduce the bias. [Neyman] [CPI Manual]

The equality uses \(E[YS]=P(S=1)E[Y\mid S=1]\). If high-volume stores respond differently to a shock, the covariance depends on the direction of the volume–inflation relationship. If the target already weights transactions by expenditure, the analogous selection question is posed under the expenditure-weighted population law.

Why randomization matters in both systems. Known probabilities support repeated-selection inference in applicable BLS stages and in the underlying Canadian rent survey. Market information and wide feeds can improve coverage in either system. Their strength depends on who is represented and how well observed prices track the target. [SC §5] [BLS design]

Sampling budget optimization

For independent strata, known stratum shares \(W_h\), pilot variation \(S_h\), and constant unit costs \(c_h\), minimize \(\sum_h W_h^2S_h^2/n_h\) under \(\sum_h c_h n_h\le B\). When sample sizes are treated as continuous and the bounds are inactive, stationarity gives \(W_h^2S_h^2/n_h^2=\lambda c_h\).

With equal costs, this is Neyman allocation since \(W_h\propto N_h\). For boxed allocations, clamp the unconstrained expression for a candidate multiplier and solve the budget equality again. Integer allocations require checking feasibility after rounding. Cost varies by stratum in this general survey-design demonstration. [Neyman]

SECTION 07

Elementary price indexes

An elementary aggregate combines comparable observations in a small product and geography cell. With matched positive prices, Carli gives equal arithmetic weight to relatives. Dutot weights relatives in proportion to base prices, so a shift from kilograms to grams for one item changes its effective weight. Jevons averages log relatives and is invariant to consistent quote-wise changes of unit. [CPI Manual] [SC §6] [BLS calculation]

AM–GM gives \(P_C\ge P_J\), with equality for identical relatives. Dutot and fixed-weight Jevons satisfy time reversal on the same matched set. Fixed-weight Jevons is transitive across periods on that set since log ratios telescope. Changing matches and weights can cause chain drift. Geometric averaging allows expenditure reallocation within a cell under a unit-elasticity model. The 2023 Canadian standard is equal-weight Jevons with component exceptions, including Dutot and transaction-based approaches. BLS typically uses a weighted geometric mean for eligible strata and a modified Laspeyres formula for specified strata. [SC §6] [BLS calculation] [CPI Manual]

Two identical offers, two formulas. Relatives of 0.90 and 1.10 give an arithmetic mean of 1.0000 and a geometric mean of 0.9950. Equal weights illustrate the Canadian Jevons formula. Source-specific weights or formula exceptions change how the same two relatives contribute in either system. [SC §6] [BLS calculation]

SECTION 09

Missing prices, replacement and quality adjustment

Temporary nonavailability may call for donor movement, carry-forward, or a component-specific rule. When a product disappears, a comparable replacement can be directly compared after size normalization when appropriate. Noncomparable replacements require explicit quality adjustment or imputation. Donor classes assume comparable movement conditional on the grouping. Rapid turnover makes comparable matches harder and delays new-product coverage. Canada documents direct price comparisons, quantity and option-cost adjustments, hedonic methods and imputation. BLS distinguishes direct comparison, direct quality adjustment and imputation such as class-mean methods in applicable components. [SC §7] [BLS data] [BLS calculation]

Given the same characteristic valuation in both periods, \(p_{\mathrm{new}}\exp[-\hat\beta^\top(x_{\mathrm{new}}-x_{\mathrm{old}})]\) estimates the new price for the old characteristics. A 500 g item at $5 replaced by 450 g at $5 has a unit-price increase of 11.11%. Unit adjustment assumes linear value in quantity. Hedonic interpretation additionally requires useful observed attributes, overlap and a reasonably stable specification.

SECTION 11

Sampling variance and statistical uncertainty

For a target \(\theta\), precision concerns the spread of \(\widehat\theta\). Accuracy also depends on how its expectation aligns with \(\theta\). In repeated-sampling exercises, center errors at their empirical mean and use divisor \(R\) for the empirical variance in this identity. Use \(R-1\) when estimating the variance of a simulation draw.

Variance under sampling designs

In SRS without replacement, \(E_p S_i=n/N\) and \(E_p S_iS_j=n(n-1)/(N(N-1))\) for \(i\ne j\). Inserting these in the covariance sum for the sample mean and using \(\sum_i(y_i-\bar y)=0\) gives the finite population correction. Independent stratified draws sum the stratum variances with squared population shares \(W_h=N_h/N\).

The design effect is \(V_{\rm design}/V_{\rm SRS}\) for a specified estimator and reference design. Within equal-size clusters of \(m\) exchangeable units with common variances and intracluster correlation \(\rho\), and independent clusters, the variance inflation for an unweighted mean is \(1+(m-1)\rho\). Unequal weights alone give \(n_{\rm eff}=(\sum w_i)^2/\sum w_i^2\) under an independent equal-variance approximation. Clustering, finite population corrections and informative weights call for their own analysis. [Neyman] [CPI Manual]

Published U.S. standard errors

For the BLS stratified random groups procedure, \(C_{A,t}\) is the full-sample cost weight for area aggregate \(A\) and a fixed item aggregate. Replace only area \(a\)'s full contribution with its replicate \(r\)'s contribution at both dates. Let \(P_{a,r}\) and \(P_f\) denote the resulting replicate and full percent changes. The handbook gives the following scaling for \(R_a\) groups in area \(a\). Certain below-stratum special indexes have no such replicates and use a separate area jackknife approach. [BLS calculation]

BLS annual tables report median standard errors for changes over one, two, six and twelve months. Treat the median as a period summary, not the standard error attached to a particular October 2026 observation. Statistics Canada explains that incomplete product frames and judgmental choices limit accurate design-based error estimation for elementary indexes. Source comparisons, quality review and component-specific evidence address other parts of the reliability question. A Canadian national standard error comparable to the BLS table is unavailable in these sources. [BLS variance] [SC §9] [BLS accuracy study]

SECTION 12

Monte Carlo comparison of sampling approaches

The simulated economy has eight regions, urban and rural outlets, chains and independents, products in several categories, and product quality and price histories. The baseline is a finite population, held fixed across replications within a scenario. A population index compares prices on each outlet’s fixed expenditure base. Monthly shocks, regional effects and outlet-specific noise interact with spending concentration. Sampling designs operate on the same target for a given stress scenario.

The probability designs compare stratified sampling and regional PPS draws followed by within-region draws. Purposive selection emphasizes visibility and spending. The hybrid combines observed transactions from large chains with a targeted sample of remaining outlets. Intervals for purposive and hybrid estimates use working variance assumptions so their simulated coverage can be inspected. The designs are stylized survey experiments.

Errors \(e_r=\widehat\theta_r-\theta\) are in percentage points. Coverage Monte Carlo standard error is \(\sqrt{\widehat C(1-\widehat C)/R}\). The corresponding standard error for mean error is \(s_e/\sqrt R\). Cost units are hypothetical. They reflect sampled outlets, geographic draws and feed acquisition assumptions.

SECTION 13

Robustness and statistical stress testing

Robustness depends on the perturbation. Additional quotes can reduce dispersion while leaving coverage bias intact. Better matches can stabilize comparable prices while delaying new products. A well-specified imputation model can handle some missingness, while loss of high-inflation units can invalidate donor assumptions.

A · Inflation shock

Large food and energy jumps test how well category movements enter the estimate.

B · Informative selection

Outlets’ price movements change with spending and visibility.

C · Geographical concentration

A shock hits one region, increasing the cost of geographic undercoverage.

D · Nonrandom missingness

High price increases are more likely to be unobserved.

E · Product turnover

Replacements introduce quality changes and possible comparability errors.

F · Stale weights

Current expenditure shares shift towards stores with higher relatives.

G · Feed concentration

Feeds overrepresent large chains and omit part of the independent market.

H · Disruption

Outage in several regions forces missing-price treatment.

For \(P=\sum_iw_ir_i\) and \(\sum_i\Delta w_i=0\), expand the perturbed product and subtract \(P\). Shifting a share \(\epsilon\) from item \(i\) to item \(j\) changes the index by \(\epsilon(r_j-r_i)\) at fixed relatives.

Compare mechanisms under a common shock. A concentrated scanner feed can miss a shock among stores outside the feed. A probability sample can still miss households or vendors outside its frame. The stress tests hold the synthetic target fixed within each scenario so these risks can be examined without attributing them to either published headline series.

Scenario results

In the baseline Python experiment, the hybrid’s 0.13 percentage point RMSE was below the stratified sample’s 0.46. Reproducibility boundaries

  1. Published growth arithmetic. Reconstruct growth directly from published levels, allowing for rounding and vintage differences.
  2. Upper-level approximation. Use compatible public components and weights, with a residual where coverage, precision or link information is incomplete.
  3. Formula reproduction. Recompute elementary, bilateral and chain indexes on fully specified examples.
  4. Design simulation. Compare survey mechanisms on a known synthetic retail population.
  5. Full production replication. Requires internal quote histories, editing rules, sampling and replicate weights, source contracts and production systems.
SECTION 18

Geographical Sampling, Regional Representation and National Aggregation

Price changes can travel unevenly across housing markets, transportation networks and retail supply chains. In each CPI, geography serves several jobs. The target geography defines whose consumption is in scope. The frame and sample determine the streams of price evidence. The aggregation areas organize prices and expenditures, and the publication areas determine the geographic detail a reader can download. A locality can be in scope, have no directly sampled outlet and still be represented in the national index.

18.1 Canada · Provinces, capitals and collection areas

The Canadian CPI covers private household consumption in urban and rural areas of all ten provinces, as well as in Whitehorse, Yellowknife and Iqaluit. Statistics Canada limits northern scope to those capital geographies because price changes there cannot be assumed to represent other communities in the three territories. The reference paper describes the three northern city areas using its primary census agglomeration terminology. The published series and weights carry the capital names. Elsewhere in the North falls outside this CPI scope. Within the provinces, the general survey selects representative collection areas. Scanner, administrative, web and centrally collected prices supply additional evidence, with coverage varying by component. Scope of the Index · Sample Strategy and Price Collection · Statistics Canada CPI FAQ.

The 2023 reference paper documents a 19-stratum index hierarchy. Ontario has four strata, Quebec three and British Columbia two. Each of the seven other provinces and the three capitals has one. The subdivisions in the three large provinces preserve geographic detail within their heterogeneous markets, using economic regions from the 2006 Census as the basis. Statistics Canada calculates stratum indexes internally and publishes the broader provincial indexes. The available program change record and 2026 basket update did not identify a replacement for the documented hierarchy by November 24, 2026. The analytical map uses the documented counts. An authoritative CPI stratum boundary or current collection-area inventory was unavailable in the public sources used here. Classifications, §4.3 · Program changes · 2026 basket update.

An economic region is a statistical grouping. A census subdivision is generally a municipality or equivalent and provides a unit to which locations in the Business Register can be assigned. A small or medium subdivision can form one collection area. Large subdivisions can be split into collection areas that combine census tracts, which are smaller census geographies. Individual outlets are then selected using the business frame, product relevance and revenue information. Statistics Canada documents population and economic activity as considerations in choosing subdivisions and market research in selecting representative offers. The publication does not supply known inclusion probabilities for every stage and pathway. The exact placement of current outlets and the scope of centrally sourced observations cannot be read from the administrative map. Reference Paper, §§5.4–5.13.

In the index hierarchy, strata \(g\in\mathcal G\) cross product classes \(k\in\mathcal K\) to form potential elementary aggregates \(\mathcal E=\{(g,k):g\in\mathcal G,\ k\in\mathcal K\}\). An elementary aggregate is the lowest class with a fixed-basket expenditure weight. Some potential cells have no class in scope, often in smaller strata. Some existing cells use geographic or product proxy movements, and the elementary detail is generally internal. The synthetic matrix lets you inspect matched prices and distinguish a cell’s expenditure share from its price movement. Classifications · Calculation.

18.4 Selection probabilities and spatial variance

For a finite population \(U\) of in-scope units, let \(U_h\) be mutually exclusive, exhaustive geographical strata with \(N_h\) units. The unit could be an eligible area, outlet, housing unit or item offer, depending on the stage. An area’s population count may serve as a frame size measure. The size measure is distinct from the expenditure share used to combine price indexes.

In a simple one-PSU probability-proportional-to-size draw within \(h\), candidate \(g\) has size \(M_g\). If the stratum is a certainty selection its inclusion probability is one. The elementary expression describes the initial, simplified design. Controlled and overlap procedures can alter actual selection probabilities, and multiple pathways into the sample require accounting for unions of events. Unknown probabilities in a nonprobability pathway cannot be recovered from the map.

For independent simple random samples without replacement within the finite strata, use the stratum sample mean \(\bar y_{h,s}\) and the within-stratum finite population variance \(S_h^2\). Spreading observations over homogeneous strata can reduce variance when price movements differ across strata. Under equal cluster sizes \(m\), exchangeable within-cluster correlation \(\rho\) and an otherwise comparable equal-weight design, the familiar approximate clustering design effect is \(1+(m-1)\rho\). Positive covariance among nearby observations means the next quote within a market can add less new information than a quote from a different market.

For spatial correlation beyond clusters, the variance of a weighted mean also depends on cross-area covariance terms \(2a_g a_j\operatorname{Cov}(\bar y_g,\bar y_j)\). Unequal cluster sizes, multistage draws, overlap and weights need design-specific treatment. With one sampled PSU per stratum, a within-stratum between-PSU variance cannot be estimated directly from that single draw. BLS uses replicate-based methods for applicable CPI estimates. Even a small collection variance can coexist with coverage error if unsampled markets price differently and donors do not track them. 18.5 Expenditure geography and national aggregation

Let \(E_{gk}^b\) be expenditure for product \(k\) in geography \(g\) expressed on basis \(b\). Once the cells partition in-scope expenditure, \(w_{gk}^b\) is a share of national expenditure, \(W_g^b\) is its geographic marginal and \(V_k^b\) its product marginal. The within-area product share \(s_{k\mid g}^b=w_{gk}^b/W_g^b\) puts a local movement on the geography’s all-items scale. Market spending matters because an identical price movement affects more purchased consumption where the relevant cell carries more expenditure.

For one fixed-quantity basket \(q^b\), the exact price change from month \(s\) to \(t\) is the weighted sum of compatible cell price changes. The weights are expenditures for those same quantities at \(s\) prices. This follows by substituting \(p_{gk,t}=p_{gk,s}R_{gk,t:s}\) in the basket cost ratio and grouping the terms by geography. The same algebra works for compatible regional subindexes within that fixed basket.

Published agency indexes use elementary formulas, price updating, aggregation rules and links across basket vintages. A 12-month change can span a basket link. Published regional rate rounding, price bases, product definitions and missing cells can also affect a reconstruction. The lab therefore uses a disclosed weight vintage and an approximate baseline from reported regional all-items rates. Compare it with the separately published national figure. For a component shock, the lab holds other category movements fixed and adds the local shock scaled by that category’s all-items share. 18.7 Spatial sampling on a known population

Each synthetic quote has a common component, a regional effect, and an outlet and quote residual. Regions have simulated population, expenditure, urban share and remoteness. The geographic effect uses \(\operatorname{Corr}(\alpha_g,\alpha_j)=\rho\exp(-d_{gj}/\ell)\) for distinct regions, with range \(\ell\) increasing with the correlation control. A chosen scenario shifts one region’s prices. The expenditure-weighted mean of all the synthetic population quotes is known. A repeated draw selects from that same population until the reader generates a new one.

Concentrated sampling picks the highest urban-density regions. The equal, population, expenditure and cost-adjusted options use the same dispersed area set to isolate allocation differences. The stratified option randomly selects one area per synthetic geographic group and expands by the inverse area selection probability when every group is represented. All strategies sample quotes without replacement within selected areas. For strategies conditioning on fixed coverage, or when a budget leaves strata without quotes, a pooled donor represents uncovered regions. Allocation controls quote counts across selected places. Selection determines which places can supply quotes.

More quotes from the same outlets can improve precision for sampled markets while leaving geographic differences unobserved. A wider spread can lower coverage error while adding costs, and the variance of an inverse-probability estimator can be high if selected areas differ greatly from others in their group. A modern measurement system

Probability selection brings explicit inferential leverage where frames and design information are practical. Large transaction feeds give more price and quantity detail where retailers participate. Rent and household spending surveys supply information that individual retailers cannot. Both statistical systems must balance continuity in matched offers against changes in the markets they measure.

A modern system could pair representative probability components with audited transaction and administrative feeds, document the uncovered domains, and use overlap to test for discrepancies. Explicit treatment of turnover, transparent imputation diagnostics, refreshable expenditure weights and released uncertainty summaries can improve different aspects of reliability. The two countries’ housing constructs are a substantive choice about the target. Descriptive empirical comparisons and scenario-specific simulations inform that choice and its consequences without yielding a single robustness ranking. [CPI Manual] [SC §9] [BLS accuracy study]
