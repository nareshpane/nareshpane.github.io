01

The price of an ordinary basket

Start with a receipt. Keep the shopping list still. Let only prices move.

If a household buys approximately the same basket of goods and services over time, how much more—or less—does that basket cost?

Begin with two cartons of milk, three loaves of bread and two kilograms of apples. At the first month’s prices, milk costs 2 × $4 = $8, bread costs 3 × $3 = $9 and apples cost 2 × $5 = $10. The basket costs $27. If every price rises by 10%, the same quantities cost $29.70. The additional $2.70 divided by the original $27 gives 0.10, or 10%.

C_0=\sum_i p_i^0q_i,\quad C_1=\sum_i p_i^1q_i,\quad \Delta\%=100\left(\frac{C_1}{C_0}-1\right)

C is total basket cost in dollars; p is a dollar price per unit; q is the number of those units; i identifies an item. Superscripts identify months, not powers.

The official Canadian CPI expands this idea across household consumption. It samples product offers, adjusts for quality differences, combines price changes within groups and then weights groups by expenditure. A household can spend more because prices rise, quantities rise, or both. Holding quantities fixed isolates the first mechanism.

02

An index is a scale, not a price

Dollar costs, relative changes and inflation rates answer different questions.

I_t=100\frac{C_t}{C_0}

Iₜ is a dimensionless index at time t. Cₜ is the cost of the fixed basket at t; C₀ is its reference cost. At the reference date Cₜ = C₀, so I₀ = 100.

Dividing costs cancels dollars. Milk cartons, kilowatt-hours and bus trips have different physical units, but their expenditure totals share a dollar unit. Indexing then provides a common scale. A basket that rises from $27 to $29.70 moves from index 100 to 110; its price change is 10%, whichever currency denomination is used.

Index reference

The scale of published levels: generally 2002 = 100. The annual average of the reference year is 100; every month in that year need not be 100.

Weight reference

The expenditure pattern supplying the basket: 2025 expenditures for the May 2026 update.

Price reference

The denominator prices for a comparison. April 2026 is the new basket’s link month. It is not a new 2002-style normalization.

A basket with eight branches

From recognizable products to a statistical classification.

Food includes goods and services. Grocery purchases and restaurant meals belong to the same major component.

The CPI covers consumption goods and services within its defined population, geographic and transaction scope. It is not a complete household balance sheet: purchases of financial assets are outside consumption, and buying a house is not treated like buying a loaf of bread.

Owned accommodation is a service flow

The Canadian approach measures costs associated with using a dwelling: mortgage interest, replacement cost, property taxes, insurance, maintenance and other owned accommodation expenses. Mortgage principal and the full acquisition price of land and housing are not simply added as current consumption. Rent also needs to distinguish pure price change from changes in dwelling characteristics.

04

The weight behind a price change

A small change in a large expense can outweigh a large change in a small one.

w_i^b=\frac{E_i^b}{\sum_j E_j^b},\qquad \sum_iw_i^b=1

Eᵢᵇ is spending on category i in expenditure reference period b. Dividing by all in-scope spending converts dollars into a share. The shares add to one before public rounding.

A category receiving $20 of every $100 has a 20% expenditure share. In a simple fixed-basket comparison, a 10% price rise in that category alone contributes 2 percentage points. This is the same expenditure logic that makes shelter consequential in the Canadian basket.

The first column of weights values the 2025 expenditure basket at 2025 prices; the second updates those prices to April 2026. Their difference is a change in percentage points, not a quantity change or a category inflation rate.

Reference-period shares describe spending at 2025 prices. Link-month shares revalue the basket at April 2026 prices. For example, food changes from 16.83% to 16.93%, shelter from 28.51% to 28.30%, and transportation from 17.87% to 18.54%. These are different objects. The displayed link shares sum to 100.01% because they are rounded; calculations below normalize them explicitly.

\widetilde E_i^{\ell b}=E_i^b\frac{P_i^\ell}{\bar P_i^b},\qquad s_i^{\ell b}=\frac{\widetilde E_i^{\ell b}}{\sum_j\widetilde E_j^{\ell b}}

ℓ denotes the link month and b the annual expenditure period. P̄ᵢᵇ is an annual average price index on a consistent scale. This describes price updating when the category has a stable basket. The official adjustment is performed at detailed levels; a broad component ratio is only an approximation.

Why divide by the annual average index? If the underlying quantities are constant over year b, annual spending equals that fixed quantity times the sum of its 12 monthly prices. Moving those quantities to the link month therefore uses the link price divided by the annual average price; the common factor of 12 cancels when shares are normalized. The Reference Paper implements this through initial values and subsequent price updating (8.17–8.18).

Arbitrary current household budgets do not supply those shares.

Whose spending determines a basket share?

The weights represent aggregate in-scope household expenditure. A household spending twice as much contributes twice as much to those expenditure totals; the method does not first give every household an equal vote. The Reference Paper calls this plutocratic weighting. Household Final Consumption Expenditure estimates support higher-level totals, while the Survey of Household Spending and supplementary sources supply detail. Neither the geometric mean of observed offers nor an average household budget is a substitute for that expenditure system.

05

From a shelf label to an observation

The target is a comparable transaction, with quantity and quality held in view.

A representative product specifies what to look for. A product offer is more concrete: a particular variety, package, outlet and set of transaction conditions. Geography and retailer selection give the observation a place in the sample. Matched-model collection tries to follow that same offer through time.

Sources and sampling

Price collection uses retail establishments, online collection, administrative records and retail scanner transactions. Sampling is adapted to each market; not every observation comes from an identical probability design. Scanner unit values can combine transactions of a sufficiently specific product, while changing the mix of products in a broad average can masquerade as a price change.

Comparability and validation

Relevant consumer taxes, product characteristics and transaction conditions matter. A missing price may require imputation from related observations. A replacement model may need an explicit quality adjustment or a comparable replacement procedure. Neither deleting every missing item nor equating a more expensive replacement with pure inflation is defensible.

Quality is part of the denominator

If a replacement phone costs $550 rather than $500 but has an estimated $40 improvement in features, a simple illustrative adjustment compares $510 with $500: 2% pure price growth. The $40 is a teaching assumption, not an official hedonic estimate. Sample a population of transactions, not an abstract list of prices

The conceptual population consists of in-scope consumption by private households, expressed through transactions involving products, sellers, places and conditions. A collected product offer is an observed representative of part of that population. Observing every transaction is infeasible; a frame, a selection procedure and a rule for maintaining comparability connect the population to the sample.

Three stages in the general collection strategy

Census subdivisions and collection areas organize geographical selection; outlet frames connect locations to product classes; representative-product specifications guide the selection of actual offers. This general framework has important exceptions.

1 · Geography

Collection areas are based on census subdivisions, with larger subdivisions split into smaller areas. Population and economic activity inform selection. A collection area is not automatically a separately published CPI geography.

2 · Outlets

The Business Register, retail commodity and service surveys link sellers to product classes. Pre-contact checks validate eligibility; subsequent outlet selection and replacement lists support ongoing collection. Revenue information helps represent the markets in which consumers buy.

3 · Products

Experts specify representative products. Assigning one to an outlet creates a target product offer; collection selects a locally relevant actual offer matching those specifications. Allocation reflects expenditure weight, variability and collection cost.

One design does not fit every product

Scanner transactions supply specific-product unit prices; administrative records and online collection may replace field collection. Cut-off sampling ranks available outlets or products to cover much of a market. Profiles hold service bundles and consumer conditions fixed; bestseller methods address selected content markets. Full-universe cases, such as a single regulated fee, need no ordinary offer sample. Rent and owned accommodation use specialized methods.

What can go wrong between months?

Missing sellers or products create coverage and selection effects. Replacements can alter quality or outlet mix; matching, explicit adjustments and imputation are then essential. An observed change may include measurement error as well as price movement. Neither a large sample nor a geometric mean resolves a systematically unrepresentative frame.

The sampling simulation in Chapter 17 isolates variation under an explicitly specified synthetic design. It does not provide a confidence interval for official CPI.

The geometry of elementary indices

Average changes before you aggregate expenditures.

r_i^{t-1,t}=\frac{p_i^t}{p_i^{t-1}},\qquad J=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}r_i\right)^{1/n}=\exp\!\left(\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log r_i\right)

n is the number of matched positive price observations. rᵢ is the price relative of observation i. J is a ratio; multiply by 100 to express a period-to-period index with a 100 reference.

Take logarithms of a product and it becomes a sum. Dividing by n averages proportional changes in log space; exponentiating returns the original scale. For relatives 1.10, 0.90 and 1.20, J = (1.188)^(1/3) = 1.0591, approximately a 5.9105% increase. The arithmetic mean is 1.0667.

C=\frac1n\sum_i\frac{p_i^t}{p_i^0},\qquad D=\frac{\sum_i p_i^t}{\sum_i p_i^0}

C is Carli, an unweighted arithmetic mean of relatives. D is Dutot, a ratio of arithmetic mean prices. Dutot implicitly gives more weight to higher base prices and requires commensurate product units.

With relatives 2 and ½, the geometric mean is 1 while Carli is 1.25. Jevons passes the time-reversal test for the same matched sample: reversing the dates produces its reciprocal. Carli generally fails it. By the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality, Carli is at least Jevons for positive relatives. Changing the unit of one product does not affect its relative, but does affect Dutot’s implicit price weights.

Jevons does not make extreme relatives harmless, and it is undefined at nonpositive prices. Equal observation weights are also not household expenditure weights. It is compatible with a unit-elastic substitution interpretation, but does not estimate actual household substitution. The standard elementary formula is often Jevons; special methods for rent, mortgage interest, replacement cost and other classes prevent a universal formula claim.

Method: Statistics Canada, CPI Reference Paper (2023), Chapter 6, equations (6.1)–(6.2), paragraphs 6.13–6.15; Appendix A. Original teaching examples and figures on this page are illustrative.

When information supports explicit elementary weights

G_w=\prod_i r_i^{\alpha_i}=\exp\left(\sum_i\alpha_i\log r_i\right),\qquad \alpha_i\ge0,\quad\sum_i\alpha_i=1

αᵢ is an explicit elementary weight. Taking logs changes the product into a weighted sum. Equal αᵢ = 1/n recovers Jevons. These weights belong inside the elementary calculation and are not automatically the eight major-component basket shares.

07

Four ways to price a basket

The choice of quantities is the decisive difference.

P_L^{0,t}=\frac{\sum_i p_i^tq_i^0}{\sum_i p_i^0q_i^0},\qquad P_P^{0,t}=\frac{\sum_i p_i^tq_i^t}{\sum_i p_i^0q_i^t}

Laspeyres (L) prices the earlier quantities q⁰ at both sets of prices. Paasche (P) prices later quantities qᵗ at both sets. p⁰ and pᵗ are the corresponding unit prices.

P_F^{0,t}=\sqrt{P_L^{0,t}P_P^{0,t}},\qquad P_{Lo}^{0,t}=\frac{\sum_i p_i^tq_i^b}{\sum_i p_i^0q_i^b}

Fisher (F) is the geometric mean of Laspeyres and Paasche. Lowe (Lo) uses fixed quantities from another period b, which need not equal 0 or t.

These formulas compare two costs of the same quantities within each ratio. Laspeyres is a special Lowe index with b = 0. The Canadian CPI uses a Lowe approach at the upper level because expenditure data arrive after their reference period; the quantities embodied in the latest available expenditure basket predate the prices being compared. Price updating bridges that timing gap.

When consumers substitute away from goods whose relative prices rise, a fixed early basket can overstate the cost of attaining the earlier utility level. Under standard optimizing-consumer assumptions, Laspeyres provides an upper bound for a base-utility cost-of-living index; Paasche relates to a different, current-utility comparison. These are conditional statements, not a guarantee that any observed Lowe value exceeds a Fisher index.

Fisher satisfies time reversal and factor reversal for matched positive data. Laspeyres and Paasche are dual when dates are reversed. Additivity for a fixed basket permits consistent aggregation with appropriate expenditure shares; rebasing and separately chain linking broad published series can remove the simple additivity of their displayed levels. The official CPI is neither a Paasche nor a Fisher index.

08

Climbing the aggregation tree

Local geometric means become expenditure-weighted relatives.

s_i^{0b}=\frac{p_i^0q_i^b}{\sum_jp_j^0q_j^b},\qquad P_{Lo}^{0,t}=\sum_i s_i^{0b}\frac{p_i^t}{p_i^0}

Multiply each share by its price relative: the pᵢ⁰ cancels, leaving pᵢᵗqᵢᵇ divided by the common reference expenditure. Summing yields the Lowe cost ratio. Shares and relatives must refer to the same basket and price reference.

For example, a 60% food branch moving by 5% and a 40% shelter branch moving by 2% give 0.6 × 1.05 + 0.4 × 1.02 = 1.038. The resulting aggregate rises 3.8%. This arithmetic weighting is different from the geometric averaging of product-offer relatives within a typical elementary aggregate.

10

The level and the speed of change

Disinflation can coexist with a rising cost of the basket.

\pi_t^{MoM}=100\left(\frac{I_t}{I_{t-1}}-1\right),\quad \pi_t^{YoY}=100\left(\frac{I_t}{I_{t-12}}-1\right)

π is percentage price growth. Month-over-month compares adjacent months; year-over-year compares the same month one year earlier. Both use index ratios, not index-point differences.

\pi_{a,b}=100\left(\frac{I_b}{I_a}-1\right),\qquad \frac{I_b}{I_a}=\prod_{t=a+1}^{b}\left(1+\frac{\pi_t^{MoM}}{100}\right)

a and b are selected months. Cumulative inflation compounds monthly ratios. Adding rounded monthly percentages is generally only an approximation.

View the plotted observations as a table

In September 2026, 170.0 − 169.8 = 0.2 index points, but 100 × (170.0/169.8 − 1) = 0.1178%. Index points and percent are distinct units. Over the year, 170.0 − 164.9 = 5.1 index points corresponds to 3.0928% inflation.

Disinflation means inflation slows. In the teaching animation, successive increases of 6%, 3% and 1% lift a 100.0 index to 106.0, 109.2 and 110.3. The price level keeps rising. Deflation means the relevant inflation rate is negative. A lower annual rate can also reflect a high comparison level a year earlier—a base effect—even if this month’s prices increased.

Separate a season from a trend

Published adjustment is an estimated statistical procedure, not subtraction of an obvious wave.

Y_t=T_t+S_t+R_t,\qquad Y_t=T_tS_tR_t\ \text{(multiplicative)}

Y is the observed series; T is trend-cycle; S is seasonal variation; R is irregular variation. Additive components share Y’s units. Multiplicative components are factors; logs convert their product into a sum.

Clothing sales, holiday travel and fresh produce availability can generate recurring patterns. Adjustment estimates the predictable seasonal component so adjacent-month changes are easier to interpret. In the synthetic additive model below, the seasonal term is known because we supplied it. In real data it must be estimated, with calendar effects, outliers and model choices.

A seasonal product is a collection problem before it is an adjustment problem

A winter jacket can disappear from a particular outlet in summer. That does not imply a zero price, and removing its expenditure weight every summer would change the basket as well as its prices. The Reference Paper describes calendar-year fixed-basket weights and out-of-season imputation for highly seasonal products. Donor movements from a broader aggregate maintain the calculation until comparable in-season observations return.

I_t^{missing}=I_{t-1}^{missing}\frac{I_t^{donor}}{I_{t-1}^{donor}}

A schematic donor-relative imputation. It assumes the missing branch follows the selected donor’s movement during the gap; actual donor selection and re-entry treatment depend on the product method.

Synthetic example: a missing branch last stood at 100.0. If its assumed donor moves from 150.0 to 153.0, the imputed branch becomes 100 × (153 / 150) = 102.0. This fills a missing movement under an assumption; it does not observe an unavailable jacket’s price. Seasonal adjustment is a separate operation on an index history after collection and construction.

Reference Paper, Chapter 10, paragraphs 10.29–10.40, especially 10.36–10.38. This explanation follows the documented 2023 method rather than asserting an independently verified 2026 operational change.

12

A rate is not a contribution

A contribution measures how much of aggregate growth comes from one branch.

A_t=\sum_i E_i^0r_i^t\ \Longrightarrow\ g=100\left(\frac{A_t}{A_0}-1\right)=\sum_i100s_i^0(r_i^t-1)

A is aggregate fixed-basket expenditure. Eᵢ⁰ is reference expenditure, sᵢ⁰ = Eᵢ⁰/A₀ and rᵢᵗ is the matching price relative. Each term is a percentage-point contribution.

c_i^{u,t}=100\frac{E_i^0r_i^u}{\sum_jE_j^0r_j^u}\left(\frac{r_i^t}{r_i^u}-1\right)

For a later comparison u → t within the same fixed basket, use shares price-updated to u, not the original sᵢ⁰. Contributions sum exactly to aggregate growth for that basket. Crossing a basket link requires consistent linked segments and detailed contributions.

Look inside the distribution

Exclusions, trimming, a weighted median and a common factor are different filters.

Headline CPI covers the full basket. Excluding gasoline, excluding energy, and excluding food and energy define alternative baskets. They remove named categories whether their prices are unusually volatile this month or not. CPI-trim and CPI-median instead use the weighted distribution of component price changes, after the required tax and seasonal adjustments.

F(x)=\sum_iw_i\mathbf1(x_i\le x),\quad m=\inf\{x:F(x)\ge0.5\},\quad \tau=\frac1{0.6}\int_{0.2}^{0.8}F^{-1}(u)\,du

xᵢ is a component monthly percentage change; wᵢ are weights summing to one. m is the weighted median. τ is the mean of the middle 60% of expenditure weight; a boundary category can be partially retained.

Inflation has a geography

Compare movements within each geography, not the apparent cost of living between them.

Different housing markets, energy prices, taxes, transportation patterns and expenditure shares can produce different regional price movements. A region’s index of 178 does not establish that it is more expensive than a region at 163: the indices track changes from their own reference baskets, not comparable dollar price levels.

Whitehorse, Yellowknife and Iqaluit are territorial city series; they do not stand for complete territorial coverage. Iqaluit’s reference is December 2002 = 100, while most displayed series use annual 2002 = 100. Ratios within a consistently based series still yield valid inflation rates. Historical coverage and component availability vary.

National CPI combines geographically weighted expenditures and price changes through the official hierarchy. An unweighted average of provincial index levels has no such interpretation. The tile arrangement is a schematic selector, not a geographic map.

How geographical aggregation works in a fixed-basket model

P_g^{0,t}=\sum_i s_{i|g}^0r_{ig}^{0,t},\qquad P_{Canada}^{0,t}=\sum_g W_g^0P_g^{0,t}

g identifies a region and i a product group. sᵢ|g is the within-region reference expenditure share; Wg is the region’s share of the same nationwide reference expenditure. Both sets sum to one. This identity assumes a common fixed basket, compatible reference prices, consistent scope and additive detailed expenditures.

Turn nominal dollars into purchasing power

Choose the dates before interpreting a “real” amount.

V_t^{\mathrm{real},b}=V_t^{\mathrm{nominal}}\frac{I_b}{I_t},\qquad V_{b\to t}^{\mathrm{equiv}}=V_b\frac{I_t}{I_b}

Vᵗ is money received or spent at date t. Multiplying by Iᵇ/Iᵗ expresses it in base-month b purchasing-power dollars. The reverse ratio gives the later nominal amount needed to match an earlier purchasing power.

OFFICIAL CPI • USER-SUPPLIED AMOUNT

Salary, pension, transfer or contract comparison

If nominal wages grow by factor N while CPI grows by factor P, real wage growth is N/P − 1, not exactly the difference between the two growth rates. At 4% nominal growth and 3% inflation, the real increase is 1.04/1.03 − 1 = 0.9709%.

CPI adjustment uses the average expenditure structure represented by the index. A household facing a rent increase, buying a first home or spending an unusually high share on health services can have a different experience. Pension, transfer and contract rules may use annual averages, lags, caps or prescribed reference months. The calculator illustrates CPI conversion and does not determine a program’s actual legal entitlement.

The one-decimal index inputs are already rounded. Extra digits in these ratios are calculation precision, not new information about the unrounded official index. For a displayed index a, the unrounded value is approximately in [a − 0.05, a + 0.05). A rate reconstructed from two displayed inputs therefore has an interval of possibilities.

100\left(\frac{a-0.05}{b+0.05}-1\right)\ \le\ \pi\ \lesssim\ 100\left(\frac{a+0.05}{b-0.05}-1\right)

The bounds follow because a/b increases with its numerator and decreases with its positive denominator. These are rounding bounds, not sampling confidence limits.

Attempt the upper-level reconstruction

\widehat I_{Sep26}=I_{Apr26}\sum_{i=1}^{8}\widetilde s_i^{Apr26,2025}\frac{I_{i,Sep26}}{I_{i,Apr26}}

The tilde denotes normalized, publicly rounded link-month shares. This is a Level C approximation using eight broad published components. It assumes their public linked relatives adequately proxy a common new-basket aggregate.

The calculation is anchored at the official April headline level; it is not an independent reconstruction of the 2002-to-April history. A cross-basket annual approximation also uses April 2025 link shares for the older segment: the old-basket relative from September 2025 to April 2026 is multiplied by the new-basket April-to-September factor. Both segments use rounded broad components.

The remaining discrepancy cannot be assigned uniquely to one cause. Published index rounding, rounded weights, aggregation at only eight components, independent chain linking of components and changes below their basic classes all limit this reconstruction. Full internal elementary weights, compatible unlinked detailed relatives, microdata, imputations, quality adjustments and estimation decisions would be needed to reproduce the official construction. A matching rounded growth rate alone would not demonstrate that those steps were reproduced.

What uncertainty does—and does not—mean

A known reconstruction difference is not a confidence interval.

Representation and measurement

Coverage, selected outlets, geography and household heterogeneity affect what the index represents. Product turnover, quality adjustment, package sizes and missing observations affect the comparison being measured. Outlet substitution and shifting expenditure patterns can make an old basket less representative even when its arithmetic is correct.

Formula, rounding and revision

Different elementary formulas embody different implicit weights and substitution properties. Published rounding limits reverse engineering. Headline unadjusted CPI generally follows a non-revision policy, subject to corrections; seasonal and core estimates can be revised. Long comparisons span many baskets, products and methods, so their meaning is broader than repricing one unchanged century-old shopping list.

Sample prices from a known artificial population

Deterministic pseudo-random draws from 240 synthetic price relatives. This is an equal-probability teaching experiment, not Statistics Canada’s sample design or a confidence interval for CPI.

The complete synthetic population has a known geometric-mean price change. A sample’s geometric mean varies with the selected offers. Increasing sample size reduces typical dispersion, though a single larger draw need not be closer. The chart compares 60 simulated estimates at the selected size and highlights the current draw. Finite-population sampling is without replacement within each draw.

The Reference Paper describes mixed sampling strategies and discusses reliability without supporting a simple universal sampling variance for the headline CPI. No official 95% interval is invented here. The −0.0705 index-point Level C discrepancy is an observed difference between two calculations. It is not an estimate of official measurement error and is not evidence that the published CPI is wrong.

From prices to inflation, connected

Ten steps, with the boundary of reproducibility kept visible.

What this study reproduces

Public index extraction, monthly and annual growth arithmetic, source-table matching, regional comparisons and CPI deflation of nominal values. The teaching models independently implement elementary formulas, fixed-basket aggregation, linking, exact synthetic contributions and weighted trimming.

What remains an estimate or a source reading

Eight-component official aggregation is approximate. Core values are read from the release. Published seasonally adjusted indices are retrieved rather than re-estimated. Unpublished product-offer construction, full quality adjustment, internal weights and core estimation are not independently replicated.

The central connection is a sequence of ratios: comparable prices give elementary change; detailed expenditure shares aggregate those changes; a link connects baskets; ratios of the linked index give inflation. Understanding where the weights, comparisons and reference periods change is more useful than memorizing a single “CPI formula.”
