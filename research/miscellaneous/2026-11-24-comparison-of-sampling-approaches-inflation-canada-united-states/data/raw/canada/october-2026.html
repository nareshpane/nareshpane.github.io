The Consumer Price Index (CPI) rose 3.1% year over year in October, matching its 3.1% increase in September.

Upward pressure on the all-items CPI in October was broad-based, stemming from higher prices for natural gas, gasoline, groceries and clothing. Conversely, a decline in prices for cell phone plans, as well as slower price growth for rent, put downward pressure on headline inflation. Excluding energy, the CPI rose 2.0% in October, after increasing 2.1% in September.

The CPI increased 0.2% month over month in October. On a seasonally adjusted monthly basis, the CPI rose 0.2%.

Gasoline prices increase at a faster pace

Year over year, prices for gasoline rose at a faster pace in October (+30.9%) compared with September (+27.8%), partly due to a base-year effect. In October 2025, crude oil prices were lower due to concerns about oversupply, leading to a 4.8% monthly decline in gasoline prices. This decrease is no longer impacting the 12-month movement, putting upward pressure on the gasoline index in October 2026.

Although gasoline prices fell month over month in October (-2.5%), price levels remained elevated amid continued uncertainty surrounding the geopolitical conflicts in the Middle East and Ukraine.

Prices for gasoline increase year over year

Price growth for groceries accelerates in October

Following four consecutive months of slower price growth, prices for food purchased from stores rose at a faster pace year over year in October (+2.0%) compared with September (+1.7%).

Driving the year-over-year acceleration in grocery prices were higher prices for fresh vegetables, up 1.4% in October after falling 2.9% in September. Price increases for sugar and confectionery (+0.4%) and condiments, spices and vinegars (+2.6%) also contributed to accelerating inflation for groceries.

Despite the acceleration in price growth, this was the third consecutive month where grocery inflation increased at a slower pace than the all-items CPI. However, compared with October 2020, prices for groceries have risen 31.9%.

Consumers pay more for clothing

After declining 0.3% year over year in September, prices for clothing rose 1.8% in October. The increase was driven by higher prices for women's clothing (+2.2%) and men's clothing (+1.4%).

Prices for clothing were up 3.4% month over month in October, the largest monthly increase since September 2019 (+5.3%). The price increase in October 2026 coincided with higher shipping costs.

Prices for cellular services decline due to a base-year effect

Prices for cellular services fell 6.2% year over year in October, following a 0.8% increase in September. Several wireless service providers increased prices on cell phone plans in October 2025, with prices rising 8.2% month over month. This increase no longer influences the 12-month price movement, putting downward pressure on the cellular services index.

On a monthly basis, prices for cellular services increased 0.6% in October 2026.

Regional highlights

Year over year, prices rose at a slower pace in five provinces in October compared with September. The deceleration in British Columbia was mainly due to a year-over-year slowdown in rent prices.

Chart 5 
The Consumer Price Index increases at a slower pace in five provinces

Natural gas prices rise year over year in Ontario

In Ontario, prices for natural gas rose 3.0% year over year in October after declining 18.7% in September. Following a one-time rate increase in July 2025, natural gas prices fell 22.4% month over month in October 2025 as prices returned to earlier levels. This decline has now fallen out of the 12-month movement, putting upward pressure on natural gas prices in Ontario in October 2026.

Offsetting the increase for natural gas prices on a year-over-year basis in October in Ontario was slower price growth for property taxes (+3.3%) and rent (+1.1%).

Property taxes rise at a slower pace

Property taxes and other special charges, which are priced annually in October, rose 3.8% at the national level, compared with October 2025 (+5.6%). Contributing to the deceleration was slower price growth in Manitoba in 2026 (+4.5%) compared with 2025 (+19.5%), when prices rose in part due to higher sewer and garbage fees in Winnipeg.

Property taxes accelerated the most in Newfoundland and Labrador, rising 5.9% in 2026, following a 2.6% gain in 2025.

Property taxes and other special charges are determined by four factors: the assessed value of homes and land; municipal and provincial tax rates; charges levied by municipalities for specific services such as wastewater and garbage collection; and homeowner tax rebates.

Explore the Consumer Price Index tools

Visit the Consumer Price Index portal to find all Consumer Price Index (CPI) data, publications, interactive tools and announcements highlighting new products and upcoming changes to the CPI in one convenient location.

Check out the Personal Inflation Calculator. This interactive calculator allows you to enter dollar amounts in the common expense categories to produce a personalized inflation rate, which you can compare with the official measure of inflation for the average Canadian household—the CPI.

Browse the Consumer Price Index Data Visualization Tool to access current (Latest Snapshot of the CPI) and historical (Price trends: 1914 to today) CPI data in a customizable visual format.
