Hypergraph Demo

A hypergraph is a generalization of an ordinary graph. In a graph, every edge joins exactly two vertices. In a hypergraph, a single hyperedge may join two vertices, three vertices, four vertices, or more.

Analytical definition.
A hypergraph is a pair H=(X,E) where X is a finite vertex set and E is a family of subsets of X. Each element of E is called a hyperedge. Thus ordinary graphs are the special case in which every hyperedge has size 2.
Algebraic notation.
If X={v1,v2,v3,v4} and E={e1,e2} with e1={v1,v2,v3} and e2={v3,v4}, then the same hypergraph may also be written as
H=(X,E), · X={v1,v2,v3,v4}, · E={{v1,v2,v3},{v3,v4}}.
How to read each PNG
Each picture shows one hypergraph in four views, ending with a simple-graph translation.
Simple-graph translation
The last panel uses the 2-section: vertices are adjacent when they lie in a common hyperedge.

How the examples are organized

The ten PNGs below are ordered from simpler to more structurally involved examples. Early examples are graph-like, while later ones show larger hyperedges, overlaps, singleton edges, and isolated vertices.

  • The folder placeholder used throughout is hypergraph_outputs/.
  • Each example lists the hypergraph algebraically before showing the PNG path.
  • The captions are intentionally short so the page stays clean and easy to scan.
